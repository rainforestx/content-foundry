## 1. Segments

- S1 PRE-AWARENESS MAJORITY. Most people who would benefit from a remote
  microphone do not know the category exists; outside school settings very
  few eligible users adopt one (communities lens). Entry-level what-is-this
  content has a larger audience than any comparison piece. They arrive from
  a symptom search, not a product search.
- S2 FITTED-AND-FORGOT WEARER. Adult NHS or private wearer who knows their
  routine and nothing about codes; identifies parts by shape and the
  red/right blue/left markers; assumes consumables come free from their
  clinic (institutions + substrate). Plain-language surface is written for
  this segment.
- S3 SELF-DIRECTED TECHNICAL OWNER. Knows their platform name, speaks in
  version numbers (V2, iN, 3), comparison-shops clinic vs online vs eBay,
  resents clinic markup, and asks worth-the-price before any feature
  question (communities). Reads opt-in depth; smells evasion instantly.
- S4 CHANNEL-IDENTIFIED WEARER. Knows the device by where it came from:
  NHS names (Nathos, Danalogic), Specsavers, Boots, Costco. Cannot name
  the manufacturer, and genuinely may not be able to - some retailer
  ranges are multi-manufacturer (substrate). Needs channel-to-ecosystem
  translation no manufacturer page provides.
- S5 PARENT OF A DEAF CHILD. Says "radio aid" (never "remote microphone");
  says "deaf" per NDCS convention, not "hearing-impaired"; approaches
  equipment as an entitlement to secure from the local authority, with
  private purchase as the fallback when provision fails or excludes home
  use (communities + institutions). Worries are operational - free play,
  the teacher forgetting to mute, child acceptance - not spec-shaped.
- S6 COCHLEAR IMPLANT USER. Assumes accessory content excludes them unless
  their processor is named; arrives with harder, processor-specific
  compatibility questions and expects more technical depth than any other
  segment (communities + substrate; matches the catalogue's CI rules).

Funding overlay, cross-cutting: working-age UK adults commonly get Roger
equipment free through Access to Work even on NHS aids; children's
provision routes through local authorities and EHC plans (communities +
institutions). Price content that ignores these routes reads as ignorant
to the very readers most likely to buy.

## 2. Belief baselines and documented misconceptions

What readers already believe, wrongly or not, before the first paragraph:

1. The receiver/licence system is the single biggest comprehension gap:
   transfer, recovery, and does-this-used-unit-still-have-licences
   questions dominate forum threads (communities). The catalogue's
   DO-NOT-SAY lists encode the same two wrong turns: RogerDirect owners
   buying receivers they do not need, and older-aid owners assuming the
   built-in path serves them (substrate).
2. Bluetooth, T-coil, FM and Roger are conflated. Some readers frame
   Bluetooth as the modern option and Roger as legacy FM; explainer pages
   exist solely to untangle this (communities); the catalogue bans exactly
   these conflations (substrate). Untangle early, keep Phonak's own
   "virtually every" hedge.
3. Self-install beliefs are polarized in both wrong directions: "anyone
   can install in 5 minutes" and "you must see an audiologist" are both
   banned claims; the truth is a path matrix (substrate; the forum asks
   it verbatim: "Does Roger On 3 need audiologist?").
4. The six-participant figure gets misread as a listener limit
   (substrate GAP4); expect readers to arrive with it.
5. Colour is trusted as an identifier where it lies (wax filter packs
   vary by region) and IS the identifier where it holds (red/right,
   blue/left markers) (substrate).
6. Price expectations: four-figure quotes trigger genuine shock; readers
   compare a clinic quote against online prices and second-hand eBay at
   40-60 percent of retail; underwhelmed first use is a documented
   failure mode when expectations were set by marketing (communities).
7. Renamed platforms create a two-name reality (Infinio / Infinio Ultra);
   owners hold whichever name they were fitted under and must not be made
   to feel obsolete (substrate).

## 3. Vocabulary per segment

- "Radio aid" leads for S5 and any paediatric context; "remote
  microphone" is the technical synonym introduced after it - the reverse
  of manufacturer copy (communities + institutions).
- "Deaf, or have hearing loss" per RNID; NDCS uses "deaf" for any level.
  Never "hearing-impaired" in parent-facing prose (institutions).
- "Marker" for ear-side colour; shape-and-mechanism language for
  consumable identification (substrate).
- Channel names are identity: name the retail model and where it was
  bought, never corporate-structure words (whitelabel, OEM) (substrate).
- S3 speaks in version numbers and licence vocabulary; matching it in
  opt-in depth signals competence (communities).

## 4. Register - what earns trust here

- Institutional ceiling: reading age 9-11 on the surface, second person,
  short imperative sentences, one-sentence functional definitions for any
  device mechanism, care-pathway terms assumed (audiologist, referral),
  gadget mechanisms always explained from zero (institutions).
- Two-tier layering: plain language carries the page; technical codes are
  opt-in, never forced - the catalogue's sec 12 rule, confirmed by how
  institutions write (substrate + institutions).
- Concession-first endorsement is the single most consistent trust move
  across every successful independent property: concede a real drawback
  (price, the limits of aids in noise, a product that failed) before any
  recommendation - the concession licenses the endorsement (register).
- Yes-first FAQ answers with a self-assessment rule of thumb, service
  offer, then soft caveat; warning-led framing only for genuine safety
  (substrate sec 15).
- Visible willingness to route the reader away: the shipped catalogue
  answers "do I need this?" with "Usually not" for the wrong audience,
  and that is its trust register made concrete (substrate). An
  independent guide does the same or loses S3 immediately.
- NHS-free-first ordering: state the free/provided route before the paid
  one, always (institutions).
- Price honesty is expected in real numbers; "worth it" must be answered,
  scoped ("for some people"), never universal (register + communities).
- Independence claims are the currency of trust, and they work only when
  tied to concrete facts (funding, procurement, disclosure), not
  adjectives (register + institutions).
- Question-stack ordering from the shipped FAQ corpus: category basics
  (is this even a hearing aid?), then routing (which mic, which receiver
  path, will it work with MY aids), then trust (genuine part, warranty)
  (substrate). Answer in that order.

## 5. Tensions requiring operator rulings

1. PRICE. This audience demands real numbers and a scoped worth-it
   verdict; the signed never-list (item 17) bars taking facts from
   retailer pages, which is where UK prices live. Resolving this needs a
   signed spec amendment defining an approved price-source class (e.g.
   dated indicative observations labelled as such), or an explicit
   decision that articles stay price-free. Currently articles are
   price-free.
2. NAMED AUTHOR. Every trusted property anchors on a named human with a
   stated stake (wearer, AuD, dispenser). Whether these articles carry a
   named author, and whose name with what stated stake, is an operator
   identity decision that shapes the register ceiling.
3. FUNDING ROUTES. Covering Access to Work and LA provision serves
   readers but adjoins the commercial interest of the connected retail
   business; how prominently to route readers toward free provision is a
   positioning call.

## 6. Use in article briefs

Each article brief names a primary segment and may name a secondary; the
producer writes the surface for the primary and the opt-in depth for S3
and S6 wherever they intersect the subject. The core job of any Roger
guide is path-routing - a self-identification flow (what aid, where from,
roughly when) before feature discussion (substrate synthesis). Article 1
(Roger On 3) was written pre-dossier; its brief-equivalent maps to
primary S3 with S6 required; a revision pass against this dossier is a
natural follow-up once signed.
